The science behind the approach

There's more going on than teaching a dog to sit.

I don't need to give away every detail of how I train to explain why it works. Good training is built around how dogs learn, what motivates them, how emotion affects behaviour, and how the human changes the environment around the dog.

01 · Learning

Behaviour follows consequences

Dogs learn from what happens immediately around a behaviour. Training becomes more reliable when the right behaviour is made worthwhile and repeatable.

02 · Emotion

State matters before skill

A dog that is frightened, frustrated or highly aroused may not be in the best state for learning. Understanding that state changes what you do next.

03 · Context

Dogs don't live in a training room

A behaviour that works in a quiet kitchen can fall apart outside. Real progress means building useful behaviour into the places and situations that actually matter.

04 · The human

Training changes both sides

The owner is part of the learning system. Clear timing, predictable routines and consistency can change what the dog experiences every day.

Research is encouraging, but it isn't magic: studies of dog ownership and behaviour have limitations, and an association does not automatically prove causation. The aim is to use the evidence intelligently alongside what we can actually observe in the individual dog.
